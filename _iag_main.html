▲ The finding that reframes this account

On 23 July 2026 IAG announced a partnership with OpenAI. IAG is one of only three global launch customers for OpenAI "Presence", its agentic AI platform. The stated scope is AI agents handling simple claims over the phone to free specialists for complex cases, with a focus on surge events like natural disasters. Implementation is targeted for the first half of IAG's FY27 — July to December 2026, i.e. right now.

That is Decagon's product description, at Decagon's use case, with a competitor's name on it, announced three weeks before our opportunity was even created.

Why it exists at all

  • The name is the clue. Sarah Brady owns Decagon's Accenture relationship — Accenture's intel was central to winning American Airlines ($7.15M ACV, her first deal).
  • On 11 August she wrote that Accenture "have also brought us into Qantas" and that she and Rodger were meeting Accenture "to duplicate this motion in other accounts."
  • IAG was created two days later. This is a partner-motion placeholder, not a worked deal.
▲ The counter-argument for staying in

IAG is running more than 90 AI solutions with over 60% of staff using AI regularly — this is not a company that buys one platform and stops. Presence is scoped to simple claims voice. That leaves policy servicing, renewals, complaints, intermediated/CGU broker support and all of New Zealand. And Decagon is one of six OpenAI Frontier preferred partners, so OpenAI is not automatically an adversary here. But somebody senior has to establish which of those two worlds we are in before more time goes into this.

The OpenAI deal, in detail

  • Announced 23 July 2026. IAG is one of three global launch customers for OpenAI Presence.
  • Scope: AI agents taking simple, non-event-related claims by phone, freeing specialists for complex work.
  • Explicit focus on natural-disaster surge — reducing wait times and front-line strain.
  • Implementation targeted 1H FY27 (Jul–Dec 2026).
  • Framed by press coverage as IAG betting on agentic AI "where conduct risk is highest."

FY26 financials

  • GWP up 7.6% to $18.4B, helped by rate increases and RACQ.
  • NPAT $1,022m, down from $1,359m.
  • Underlying profit around A$1.58B.
  • Final dividend 20.0cps; full year 32.0cps (from 31.0cps).
  • Ambition 2030 strategy launched; technology platform transformation continuing.

Regional competitive context

  • Sierra has signed Macquarie Bank — described internally as the first AI-native deal of scale in Australia.
  • Internal concern that regulated industries (finance, insurance) stay hard to win until data processing can be kept onshore.
  • Netomi, via Accenture's investment, is reportedly being positioned against us.
  • A prospect DJ's colleague spoke to — a health insurer — led with a data-residency question. Expect the same here.
▲ The strategic read

IAG is not AI-shy — it is AI-saturated: 90+ solutions, 60%+ staff adoption, and a flagship OpenAI partnership. That cuts both ways. The build-versus-buy conversation is already won, which removes our usual first obstacle, but the front-door voice use case is taken. Our angle is the estates the announcement does not cover, and the one thing OpenAI Presence has not publicly claimed: guardrails and conduct-risk control in a regulated industry — which is what Watchtower exists for, and which the press has already framed as IAG's central concern.
