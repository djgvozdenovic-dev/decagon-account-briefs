▲ What has already been tried

The subscription estate — where the volume is

  • Stan — approximately 2.4 million subscribers, revenue up 15% to $282.7M, record EBITDA of $36.6M, up 24%.
  • Publishing subscriptions — digital subscriptions up 17% across The Sydney Morning Herald, The Age and The Australian Financial Review.
  • 9Now — daily active users up 22%, streaming minutes up around 70%.
  • Three separate subscriber bases, three separate billing and account-management workloads, one company.

Why subscriptions are the right pitch

  • Churn is a service problem. Cancellation flows, failed payments, password resets and device limits are the top contact drivers in every streaming business.
  • News subscriptions add paywall access, tier changes and print-delivery issues on top.
  • These are high-volume, low-complexity, highly automatable — the strongest possible fit — and they are directly tied to retained revenue, not just cost.
  • Stan is growing revenue 15% with record EBITDA, so it has both budget and a margin incentive.

◆ Zach — you have first-hand knowledge nobody wrote down

Explain why the first two attempts died

Two opportunities closed in five months with no disqualification reason recorded on either. You owned the first one; Van Ulrich owned the second.

  • If it died on budget or timing, a subscription-led re-entry is worth running now.
  • If it died because Nine has an in-house build or an incumbent contract, that changes the plan entirely.
  • Either way, a third attempt without knowing why the first two failed is how accounts get burned.
2
◆ Spencer — reframe the target

Approach Stan as its own account, not as part of Nine

Stan is a ~2.4 million subscriber streaming business with its own P&L, its own growth story and record EBITDA. It behaves like a standalone company and should be approached like one.

  • The previous attempt was trying to multithread out to Stan and 9Now from the broadcast conversation. Invert it — start with the subscription business, and let it pull the group in.
  • The same applies to Nine Publishing: three national mastheads with growing digital subscriptions is a distinct servicing estate.
  • Matt Stanton is Nine's CEO — appointed permanently in March 2025, having been chief strategy and financial officer before that. A CEO with a finance and strategy background responds to retained-revenue arguments, which is exactly what churn reduction is.
3
◆ Either of you — a cross-account pattern worth using

Subscription media is now a segment, not one account

Nine has Stan, 9Now and three paywalled mastheads. Foxtel has Kayo, BINGE and Hubbl with 4.7 million subscribers. Both are on DJ's list; both were disqualified.

  • That is two Australian subscription-media groups with the same support profile and the same failed sales approach.
  • A single well-built subscription-churn proof point would reopen both — and Decagon's existing streaming and media references are the assets to lead with.
  • Worth deciding whether this is a segment play rather than two separate retries.
