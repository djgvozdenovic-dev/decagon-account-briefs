▲ What the 17 August call established

Contact is John Gurrisi, Head of Rider Experience & Product Enablement — four years at Neuron, previously Samsung. They handle 30,000–33,000 support tickets a month across five markets (Australia, Indonesia, Malaysia, Turkey and South Korea), with South Korea alone at 40–50% of volume. Current AI containment runs 65–80%, with roughly 10% to BPO, 5% to team leads and 5% escalating to John himself. Priority use cases: general inquiries, vehicle issues, scripts and payments — with payment reviews deliberately kept human for now.

The specific pain, and it is a good one

  • Translation quality is the stated problem — especially Turkish, where Microsoft Copilot misdetects the language.
  • That is a very precise, demonstrable failure. Multilingual handling across Korean, Turkish, Bahasa and English in one agent is a straightforward proof point.
  • Decision dynamics are already mapped: John evaluates on simplicity and ease of use; the CEO evaluates on cost. No metric target has been set.
  • The internal risk is written down: the deal stalls at demo and John defaults to renewing his existing contracts.

The administration is a genuine risk to the deal

  • No opportunity exists in Salesforce — on a deal with an eight-week window.
  • The account is named "Neuron APK" against neuron.sg, and is owned by Decagon RevOps.
  • The single contact, John Gurrisi, is owned by Ava Rhodes — a third party to the deal.
  • No Slack channel exists. Compare Woolworths and Telstra, which both have dedicated channels for slower-moving deals.
  • Two unrelated "Neuron" accounts also sit in Salesforce — Neurons Lab (London consulting) and The Neuron (US media) — so anyone searching will find the wrong record.
● And the company is far bigger than our records suggest

Neuron completed a merger with Beam Mobility on 22 September 2025, forming Neuron Mobility Holdings — around 100,000 e-scooters across 100 cities, and the clear market leader in Asia-Pacific. Both brands survive, each focused on its strongest markets. Our records show 120–150 staff and $24.6–37.5M revenue; those figures are pre-merger and now materially understate the business.

  • GSR Ventures and Square Peg Capital co-led both the Series A ($18.5M, December 2019) and the Series B ($43.5M, April 2022). EDBI — Singapore's Economic Development Board investment arm — joined the Series B. Genesis Alternative Ventures and Rapzo Capital are also on the register.

  • Note the timing: the last major round was April 2022 — over four years ago. Investors four years into a position, in a consolidating sector, pay close attention to cost lines.

◆ Zach or Jesse — founder-to-founder, and it solves a stated problem

Get above John before mid-October

DJ's own action from the 17 August call was to "get above John to understand his manager." The structure is now public: Zachary Wang, Neuron's co-founder, is Chairman and Global CEO of the merged company; Alan Jiang, Beam's co-founder, is CEO of Asia — and Asia is where 40–50% of the support volume sits.

  • The call notes say the CEO evaluates on cost. So the cost conversation has to happen with the CEO, not through a Head of Rider Experience.
  • Both are founder-CEOs of a scaled startup — the most natural possible audience for a Jesse or Karim note, and completely different from approaching an ASX executive.
  • This directly de-risks the logged failure mode: if the only relationship is John, and John's default is renewal, the deal dies quietly in October.
  • Alan Jiang is arguably the better target — he owns the Asian markets that generate most of the tickets, including the Korean volume Neuron inherited.

Their current state

  • 65–80% AI containment already — they are not AI-naive.
  • ~10% goes to BPO, ~5% to team leads, ~5% escalates to the department head.
  • Microsoft Copilot in the stack, and it misdetects Turkish.
  • Payment reviews deliberately kept human.
  • All vendor contracts expire mid-October 2026.

Why we can win

  • Multilingual is a demonstrable head-to-head. Korean, Turkish, Bahasa and English in one agent, tested on their own transcripts.
  • Post-merger consolidation gives a reason to change rather than renew.
  • A live NDA process is already in motion for transcript data.
  • The N4 launch shows a company already pursuing lower operating costs — the CEO's evaluation lens.

Why we could lose

  • The logged risk: John stalls at demo and renews. Containment at 65–80% is good enough to defend.
  • Internal read is "no acute pain".
  • No cost baseline, so no cost case for a cost-driven CEO.
  • Only one relationship, three or more levels below the decision-maker.
  • Eight weeks, and no opportunity record to drive urgency internally.
▲ The strategic read

This is a smaller deal than Woolworths or Telstra, and it is the one most likely to actually close in this calendar year — because a contract expiry is the only genuine deadline anywhere in DJ's book. It is also the cleanest technical win available: a named incumbent failing at a specific, testable task. The whole risk is organisational, not competitive. One relationship, no opportunity record, no cost baseline, and eight weeks. Fix the administration and get a founder to Wang or Jiang, and this is winnable.
