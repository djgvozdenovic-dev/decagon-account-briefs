4.7 million subscribers, and not one contact in our CRM. Foxtel is the largest subscription base on DJ's entire list — bigger than Stan, bigger than nib's membership, bigger than anything except the supermarkets' customer counts.

Two things make it interesting to you. First, it has a new owner: DAZN completed a $3.4bn acquisition in April 2025, and Foxtel continues as a standalone business — new owners review every cost line and every vendor. Second, the streaming brands are the wedge: Kayo, BINGE and Hubbl are subscription products where churn, billing and access issues drive nearly all contact volume.

The DAZN acquisition is the reason to come back. The previous opportunity was disqualified in May 2026 — thirteen months after DAZN took over, which means it was attempted into an organisation still absorbing new ownership. Post-integration is a better moment than mid-integration.

▲ The ownership change, verified

DAZN completed its acquisition of Foxtel Group on 2 April 2025 from News Corp, in a deal reported at $3.4 billion, acquiring the streaming platforms Kayo and BINGE along with it. Foxtel continues to operate as a standalone business within DAZN, with Foxtel, Kayo Sports, BINGE and Hubbl all retaining their brand identities. Patrick Delany is CEO of Foxtel Group; Shay Segev is CEO of DAZN. Salesforce already has the parent set correctly to DAZN — one of the few hierarchy fields that is right.

The subscription estate

  • 4.7 million subscribers across the group.
  • Kayo Sports — sports streaming. Sport drives extreme, event-driven contact spikes: a finals night or a big fight generates concentrated access and playback failures.
  • BINGE — entertainment streaming, so churn and billing dominate.
  • Hubbl — aggregation hardware and platform, which adds device and setup support.
  • Foxtel — traditional subscription TV, with installation, faults and retention calls.
  • Four products, four different contact profiles, one subscriber relationship.

▲ Why "chat" was the wrong scope

The dead opportunity was named "NB – Chat" — a channel-scoped deal. For a business with 4.7 million subscribers across four products, chat alone is a small slice of a very large problem, and it invites a channel-by-channel comparison against whatever they already run. The subscription lifecycle — cancellations, failed payments, access and device issues — is the story, and it spans every channel including voice.

Play 1 is the one only you can run. DAZN is a privately held international sports-streaming group that has just spent $3.4bn on an Australian business — that is a company with a global support estate of its own and an owner-level view of cost. 1
◆ Spencer — go one level above the account

Treat DAZN as the target and Foxtel as one of its assets

DAZN is a global sports-streaming business led by Shay Segev. It now owns Australia's largest subscription-television group and runs its own international streaming operation.

  • A DAZN-level conversation covers Foxtel, Kayo, BINGE and Hubbl plus DAZN's own subscriber base — far more than an Australian account.
  • New owners audit vendors. Thirteen months in is exactly when a group starts standardising platforms across a newly acquired business.
  • DAZN is privately held and backed by significant capital, so there is an owner to persuade rather than a public register to work.
2
◆ Zach — re-enter with a bigger scope

Come back on subscription lifecycle, not chat

The last attempt was scoped to chat and died. Patrick Delany runs Foxtel Group and is the person to reach for a group-scoped conversation.

  • Lead on churn and retention across 4.7 million subscribers, not on deflecting chat volume.
  • Kayo's event-spike problem is the demo. Sports streaming concentrates enormous contact volume into a few hours — the classic case you cannot staff for and can only automate.
  • Ask why the May opportunity was disqualified before re-approaching. Grace Kuester owned it and recorded no reason.
3
◆ Either of you — pair it with Nine

Run subscription media as a segment

Foxtel has 4.7 million subscribers across four products. Nine has Stan at ~2.4 million plus three paywalled mastheads. Both are on DJ's list. Both were disqualified without a recorded reason.

  • Same support profile, same failure pattern, same fix: one credible subscription-churn proof point reopens both.
  • Between them that is over seven million Australian subscription relationships — a segment, not two accounts.
  • Worth deciding whether Decagon builds a subscription-media reference deliberately rather than retrying each account in isolation.
