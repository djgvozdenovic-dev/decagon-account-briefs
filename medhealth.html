This is the only account in DJ's book with quantified pain, a documented decision process, a CFO champion and customer-produced ROI. It is also the only one where the referral programme's investor route genuinely applies. And it is being run as a $150K Australian pilot.

● The ownership chain nobody has followed

MedHealth Group Pty Ltd is wholly owned by ExamWorks AP, Inc, which is wholly owned by ExamWorks Group, Inc — headquartered in Atlanta. ExamWorks was acquired in June 2021 by CVC Capital Partners, with Leonard Green & Partners and GIC (Singapore's sovereign wealth fund) retaining significant stakes alongside management. MedHealth sits inside the ExamWorks global network across Australia, the US, Canada and the UK, delivering more than 2 million medical opinion and case management services a year.

Four accounts in a row have been ASX-listed with no venture register, killing the investor route every time. This one is private-equity owned. Tier 3 of Decagon's referral programme finally applies — and the prize behind it is not $150K.

Why this deal is genuinely good

  • The pain is quantified, in their numbers: 60–83% of referrals drop off the waitlist within a week; heavy Google Ads spend not converting; clinicians underutilised because intake is the bottleneck; 1.5–2 offshore FTEs doing nothing but manual triage.
  • The CFO is the champion. Pohlee Chan sent us updated use cases and dollarized ROI on 4 August — the customer did our business case.
  • The decision process is written down — the only account where that is true.
  • Ben O'Neill (Group Executive) is the economic buyer and re-engaged as of today.
  • It is at S2 Scoping — no other deal has got past S1.

Why it is stuck anyway

  • The next step has been overdue since 8 August. The reply and deck were "in progress" and remain so.
  • MedHealth has just centralised all technology buying under Brett Ferguson (EGM Digital Innovation & Engineering) — business units can no longer evaluate or buy independently. We have zero relationship with him.
  • Rob Farmer (COO) has surfaced as a second gatekeeper of technology investment. Also no relationship, and not in the CRM.
  • Despite being at S2, no POV has been presented to the economic buyer or the champion.
  • Ben is "chatting with the tech team independently" — which could be sponsorship or could be us being routed around.
▲ Liz's read, and it is the right one

Her guidance on the account was "go slow to go fast, position for a group-wide opportunity not just AAA" — AAA being Ability Action Australia, one brand of thirty. She was arguing for MedHealth group scope. The ownership chain says go further: the natural scope is ExamWorks.

▶Deal intel & competition The most specific use case we have anywhere◐ Build risk
◆ For Spencer
& Zach

The ROI is not deflection savings — it is recovered revenue from referrals that leak after paid acquisition. That reframes the price conversation entirely.

The workflow we are replacing

  • Validate whether a participant is a duplicate.
  • Chase missing referral fields by email.
  • Match clinician skills held in ScheduleO against participant needs held in MediBridge — two separate systems.
  • Create five appointment blocks manually.
  • SMS outbound is live, but their CRM cannot receive replies — so the loop never closes.
  • All of it consuming 1.5–2 offshore FTEs.

What it costs them

  • 60–83% of referrals drop off the waitlist within one week.
  • Heavy Google Ads spend not converting — they are paying to acquire referrals that then leak.
  • Clinician underutilisation caused directly by the intake bottleneck.
  • This is why the CFO is the champion: it is a revenue-leakage problem, not a support-cost problem.

Competitive picture

  • Competitor logged only as "Other".
  • Real risk is internal build — Pohlee is meeting MedHealth's in-house AI people and Richard Hawker.
  • Brett Ferguson's title is literally Digital Innovation & Engineering — a function with an incentive to build.
  • No third-party vendor has been named by the customer at any point.
● Why this is a strong Decagon use case

It is outbound voice plus inbound triage across two disconnected systems — exactly the multi-system, multi-step orchestration that a scripted bot cannot do and an internal team will underestimate. And the ROI is not deflection savings, it is recovered revenue from referrals that currently leak. That reframes the price conversation completely: a $150K pilot against 60–83% drop-off on paid-acquisition referrals is trivially justified.
