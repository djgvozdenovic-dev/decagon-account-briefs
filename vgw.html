Read the fourth tile. VGW sits on DJ's target list showing $79M of revenue. The real figure is $7.3 billion, with $657M of net profit. This is quietly one of the largest and most profitable companies on the entire list — a Perth-headquartered business earning more than TEG, nib, MedHealth, The Star and Neuron combined — and Decagon has never created an account for it.

▶Where we are The biggest data error in the whole book■ Badly mis-sized
◆ For Spencer
& Zach

The useful thing here is simply scale. A $7.3B consumer business with millions of North American players, run from Perth, with no customer-service or customer-experience executive visible anywhere in its leadership. Either that function is buried, or it is outsourced — and either answer is a good opening question.

▲ Correct the number before anyone quotes it

ZoomInfo — and therefore DJ's target list — records VGW at 554 employees and $79.3M revenue. The published figures for the financial year ended 30 June 2025 are $7.3 billion of revenue, up 19%, and net profit of $656.7 million, up around 34%. Headcount was 1,117 as at 30 June 2026. Chumba Casino alone accounts for $5.2 billion of the revenue. The list figure is out by roughly ninety-two times.

What the business is

  • Founded in Perth, operating since 2012. Describes itself as an interactive entertainment company delivering free-to-play games.
  • Social casino with sweepstakes-based promotions, aimed principally at North American players.
  • Brands: Chumba Casino, Global Poker, LuckyLand Slots, LuckyLand Casino and Monopoly Match.
  • Two in-house game studios: Golden Feather Studios and Hot Sauce Studios.
  • Malta operation as well as Perth — there is a VP Operations & MLRO (Malta) on the leadership team.

Why it should matter to Decagon

  • Millions of consumer players in North America on a freemium model — high-volume, low-value-per-contact support, which is the shape automation suits best.
  • Sweepstakes gaming carries heavy verification, redemption and compliance workload — identity checks, prize redemption, payment queries.
  • They already run a VP of Risk Operations and a VP Government Relations, so regulatory exposure is a standing concern.
  • Extremely profitable — a 9% net margin on $7.3B means budget is not the obstacle.
  • Perth-headquartered, so a genuinely local Australian account for DJ.
▲ And the gap in their org chart is the opportunity

Across the 25 most senior VGW people I could identify, there is a Chief Technology Officer, a Chief Product & Revenue Officer, a Chief Growth & Strategy Officer, a Chief Marketing Officer, two VPs of Engineering, a VP of CRM and a VP of Risk Operations — and no Chief Customer Officer, no head of customer service and no customer-experience title of any kind. For a business serving millions of players, that is either a function hidden below VP level or one handled by a third party. Finding out which is the first discovery question.

▶Ownership & governance — read before outreach Founder-controlled · going private · leadership in transition◐ Handle with care
◆ For Spencer
& Zach

There is no cap table to work here — this is a founder-controlled company taking itself private. That removes the investor route entirely and makes the founder's own network the only real path. It also means the governance situation below is directly relevant to how, and whether, you approach right now.

▲ Ownership

VGW was founded and is controlled by Laurence Escalante, a former financial planner who became a billionaire from the business. Reporting through 2026 describes VGW going private via a buyout led by Escalante, having posted record results beforehand. There is no institutional venture or private-equity register to route through — which is unusual on DJ's list and means Tiers 2, 3 and 5 all fall away.

▲ Leadership is in transition, and there is a live legal matter

Escalante has stepped down from his directorships at VGW and at his Lance East family office following an arrest on 26 January in connection with a domestic-violence and drug-related incident. He strongly denies the allegations. Mats Johnson, a long-standing associate, is reported to be steering the business as acting chief executive.

How to handle this: it is a live legal matter and an allegation that is being contested. It belongs in this brief only because it explains why there is an acting CEO and why decision-making authority is unclear right now — it is not a topic to raise with anyone at the company, and it should not appear in any outbound material. Treat the practical consequence as the only relevant part: confirm who currently holds authority before investing in a pursuit.

▲ What that means commercially

Companies in the middle of a take-private plus an acting-CEO period usually do one of two things: freeze discretionary spend, or push hard on margin ahead of the transaction. Given VGW's profitability is already exceptional, the more likely posture is efficiency and control rather than retrenchment — but that is an assumption, and it is worth testing with a low-cost approach before committing founder time.

▶Deal intel & competition Exceptional economics, unknown support estate? Unknown field
◆ For Spencer
& Zach

The angle that will land: they make roughly nine cents of net profit on every dollar of a $7.3 billion business, and they are about to be owned outright by one person. Efficiency arguments are extremely persuasive in that setting — there is no public market to explain a cost line to, only an owner.

The economics

  • Revenue $7.3B, up 19% (FY to 30 Jun 2025).
  • Net profit $656.7M, up around 34%.
  • Chumba Casino alone: $5.2B.
  • 1,117 employees generating that — extraordinary revenue per head.
  • Record results posted ahead of the buyout.

Where the support workload comes from

  • Sweepstakes model — identity verification and prize redemption are mandatory, high-friction and high-volume.
  • Freemium means a very large player base relative to paying customers, so contact volume scales faster than revenue.
  • Poker disputes and account issues on Global Poker.
  • Two regulatory jurisdictions plus North American state-level scrutiny.
  • A dedicated VP Government Relations and VP Risk Operations confirm this is managed as a risk function.

What we do not know

  • Who owns customer support, and whether it is outsourced.
  • Any contact volume or cost figure.
  • Whether the take-private has completed.
  • Who holds spending authority during the acting-CEO period.
  • Whether the $7.3B figure is AUD or USD.
▲ The strategic read

On revenue, VGW is arguably the largest opportunity on DJ's entire list — and it has been sitting there labelled as a $79M company. The reasons for caution are governance, not commercial: an acting chief executive, a take-private in progress, and no visible customer-service function to sell into. The right move is a low-cost, professional approach now to establish who owns player support, and a serious pursuit once leadership settles. The one thing not to do is leave it mis-sized on the list.
