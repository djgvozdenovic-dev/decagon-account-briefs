There is a better target in the same sector, and it is already on DJ's list.

▲ The state of the business

Bally's Corporation and Bruce Mathieson's Investment Holdings put A$300M in to take a combined ~61% stake and save Star from administration. Regulatory probity approval came through in November 2025. Material uncertainty remains over the group's ability to continue as a going concern, with three milestones outstanding: exiting the Destination Brisbane Consortium, awaiting judgment in civil proceedings brought by AUSTRAC, and restoring suspended casino licences in New South Wales and Queensland.

Why now is the wrong moment

  • The core licences are suspended. Until they are restored, the revenue base is impaired and discretionary technology spend is close to zero.
  • Four CEOs in three years. Steve McCann resigned in December; the CFO resigned effective immediately after a one-year tenure; the Group COO left at the end of January 2026.
  • Bally's chairman Soo Kim publicly described Star's problems as "the greatest bit of mismanagement I've ever seen". New owners in that frame of mind cut, they do not buy.
  • The org chart is a remediation structure, not a growth one. See below — it is dominated by risk, compliance, surveillance and financial crime.

The genuinely striking finding

  • ZoomInfo returned 97 contacts at Star. Among the 25 most senior, there is no customer service, contact centre or customer experience title at all.
  • What there is: two General Managers of Culture & Ethics Oversight, GMs of Surveillance (twice), Investigations, Financial Crime Operations, Risk & Compliance, Asset Protection & Operational Resilience, and Property Risk & Compliance.
  • That is what a company under a regulatory microscope looks like from the inside. It tells you what they will and will not fund.
● The better casino target is already on DJ's list

Crown is owned by Blackstone — acquired through SS Silver II Pty Ltd, scheme implemented 24 June 2022 for AU$8.87bn, Blackstone's largest investment in Asia Pacific at the time. Crown Melbourne's licence suitability was confirmed by the Victorian regulator in March 2024, and Blackstone's global co-head of real estate has described Crown as a long-term investment. So: a stable, committed, deep-pocketed owner with restored licences — versus a suspended operator in a rescue. And Blackstone is named explicitly in Decagon's own referral framework as a PE ecosystem target. Crown Melbourne sits on DJ's list carrying a ZoomInfo shell record of 45 staff; it deserves the attention Star is currently getting.

◆ Spencer — the account is Bally's, not Star

Treat Bally's Corporation as the target and Star as one of its properties

Bally's is a US-listed casino and gaming group that now controls Star. Its chairman Soo Kim and president George Papanier sit on the Star board, and Bally's has installed its own people — the Group CFO is now Charles Diao, appointed after Frank Krile's resignation.

  • Bally's runs a substantial portfolio of casinos and an online business in its own right. That is a customer-support estate with a US decision-maker.
  • A conversation with Bally's is about their whole portfolio, of which Star is the newest and most troubled asset — a much better framing than "we would like to sell software to a company in a rescue."
  • Soo Kim is an investor by background. Your team's network is far more likely to reach him than to reach a Brisbane GM.
  • And if Bally's is buying, Star becomes an implementation rather than a sale.
2
◆ Spencer & Zach — reallocate the effort

Swap Star for Crown, and route through Blackstone

Same sector, same use case, radically better situation. Crown has a restored Melbourne licence (VGCCC, March 2024), Blackstone as a committed long-term owner, and three major properties in Melbourne, Perth and Sydney.

  • Blackstone is one of the PE houses named in Decagon's referral framework — this is precisely the Tier 3 motion, and it is available here rather than theoretical.
  • Crown Melbourne is already on DJ's target list, but with a ZoomInfo shell record showing 45 staff and $13.5M revenue, so nobody has looked at it seriously.
  • Casinos and integrated resorts are a strong Decagon shape: bookings, loyalty, hotel service, responsible-gambling interaction — all high-volume, all guardrail-sensitive.
3
◆ Nobody, for now — but set a trigger

Watch for licence restoration, then move fast

Star is not permanently uninteresting. It is uninteresting until three things resolve, and all three are public and dateable.

  • NSW and Queensland casino licences restored — the single clearest buy signal.
  • AUSTRAC civil proceedings judgment — removes the largest contingent liability.
  • Destination Brisbane Consortium exit completed — removes the biggest capital drag.
  • When licences come back, a Bally's-controlled turnaround with 8,000 staff and no visible customer-service function becomes a genuinely attractive greenfield. Set a reminder rather than a sequence.

▶Deal intel & competition Three public milestones to watch◐ Set a trigger
◆ For Spencer
& Zach

Three public, dateable unlocks to watch: licence restoration, the AUSTRAC judgment, and the Destination Brisbane exit. Set a reminder rather than a sequence.

The three unlocks

  • Casino licences restored in NSW and Queensland. The clearest buy signal there is.
  • AUSTRAC civil proceedings judgment. Removes the largest contingent liability.
  • Exit from the Destination Brisbane Consortium completed. Removes the biggest capital drag.
  • All three are public, dateable events. Watch rather than chase.

The one angle that does work

  • Star's entire executive attention is on regulatory suitability.
  • Australian responsible-gambling rules require operators to evidence customer interactions and self-exclusion compliance.
  • That is a Watchtower-shaped argument: provable, auditable guardrails on every customer conversation.
  • It is the only pitch that speaks to what they are actually being judged on — and it is the same argument that applies to Entain and Crown.

What we do not know

  • Whether Bruce Mathieson Jnr is chair, CEO, or has held both.
  • Whether Charles Diao is a Bally's appointee — his arrival timing suggests it, but I have not confirmed the link.
  • Any contact volumes across hotels, gaming floors or loyalty.
  • Whether Star runs any conversational AI today.
  • The current licence position of Crown Perth and Crown Sydney.
▲ The strategic read

Casinos and integrated resorts should be a good Decagon vertical — hotel bookings, loyalty, event enquiries and gaming-floor service are high-volume and guardrail-heavy, and responsible-gambling obligations make auditability a compliance requirement rather than a nice-to-have. The sector is right; this particular company is two or three catalysts away from being buyable. Put the effort into Crown, keep a watch on Star, and take the Bally's conversation because it is worth having regardless.
