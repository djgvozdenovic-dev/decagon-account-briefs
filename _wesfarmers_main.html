▲ The two-part answer on how Wesfarmers buys

Part one: divisions own their operations. Managing Director Rob Scott has said it plainly — "it's quite understood that we operate on a divisional autonomy model." Bunnings, Kmart Group, Officeworks, WesCEF and Wesfarmers Health each run their own P&L and make their own operational and personnel decisions. There is no monolithic corporate technology mandate.

Part two, and this is the important bit: the group has deliberately built a central customer-data and AI capability anyway. It is called Wesfarmers OneDigital, and it owns OnePass, the Advanced Analytics Centre, the group shared data platform and the group retail media network. Alongside it, Wesfarmers Limited itself now carries an AI Technology Head and a GenAI Acceleration & Innovation Head.

So a group-level engagement is not a dead end. It just is not a purchasing mandate. Group buy-in here works as endorsement and standard-setting, which divisions then adopt — which is precisely the outcome DJ is after, and it is achievable because the central function already exists and already owns the customer data.

Where the company actually sits

  • Head office is Perth — confirmed. Registered office and principal place of business: Level 14, Brookfield Place Tower 2, 123 St Georges Terrace, Perth, Western Australia. ZoomInfo independently lists Perth, WA.
  • The company began life as a Western Australian farmers' co-operative, which is why the holding company is still in Perth while most of the retail divisions are not.
  • But the divisions are in Melbourne. Bunnings in Burnley, Kmart in Mulgrave, Officeworks in Chadstone — all Melbourne. Wesfarmers Health and WesCEF are in Perth.

Only the Perth entities — group head office, Wesfarmers Health and WesCEF — need separate travel or video.

Ownership — widely held, nobody in control

  • No controlling shareholder. ASX-listed, effectively full free float, no dual-class structure and no government stake.
  • Largest holders are global index managers: Vanguard around 9.4% and BlackRock around 6.1%, with State Street and major Australian super funds typically in a 3–8% disclosed range.
  • Roughly 68% institutional, 18% retail, 1–2% insiders as at end-2025, plus around half a million retail holders.
  • Market capitalisation has been reported in the A$70–85bn range over 2024–25.
  • What this means for your team: there is no sponsor or founder to route through. The realistic hooks are index-manager stewardship teams, Australian super funds, and — most usefully — the board itself.
