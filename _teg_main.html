A distressed asset with a brand-new technology CEO. They lost Venues NSW to Ticketmaster and Melbourne Park to AXS, cut 5.5% of staff, and borrowed A$100M to bid for contracts back. That is an offensive turnaround, not a wind-down.

TEG owns Ticketek — Australia's largest ticketing platform, and the local equivalent of Ticketmaster. Decagon has never spoken to them. But almost everything about TEG's current situation points the same way: new leadership, real financial pressure, a private-equity owner that wants an exit, and a competitive fight where service quality is the product.

▲ What has happened to this business in the last twelve months

Ticketek lost the Venues NSW contract to Ticketmaster — that is the SCG, Accor Stadium, CommBank Stadium, Allianz Stadium and Penrith Stadium — and then lost Melbourne Park to AXS, covering Rod Laver Arena, AAMI Park, John Cain Arena and Margaret Court Arena. Reports put the two contracts at around 6% of group revenue. Their CEO left after 14 months. The new CEO began a restructure that cut ~44 roles, about 5.5% of the workforce. In August they raised A$100M from lenders — who were reportedly already owed around A$1B — explicitly to bid for upcoming contracts.

Why this is a good moment, not a bad one

  • Cameron Hoy became Group CEO on 1 June 2026 — and his background is Data Science, Analytics, Digital, Product and Technology, plus running TEG's global ticketing operations. A technology executive, three months into a turnaround.
  • He is simplifying the operating model and has just removed 5.5% of the people. That is a "do more with less" mandate in writing.
  • They have new money specifically to win contracts back — this is an offensive turnaround, not a wind-down.
  • Silver Lake has been weighing an exit since 2022. An owner preparing to sell wants margin and a growth story.

Why the usual pitch is the wrong one

  • TEG's problem is not support cost. It is competitive survival against Ticketmaster and AXS.
  • Venue contracts are won on fan experience and service at scale — and they have just cut staff while trying to win bids.
  • So the pitch is AI service capability as a competitive weapon in tenders, not a back-office saving. "Here is how you promise a venue better fan support than Ticketmaster, with fewer people."
  • That framing is unique to this account on DJ's list — everywhere else we are selling efficiency.

What they lost

  • Venues NSW → Ticketmaster. Sydney Cricket Ground, Accor Stadium, CommBank Stadium, Allianz Stadium, Penrith Stadium.
  • Melbourne Park → AXS. Rod Laver Arena, AAMI Park, John Cain Arena, Margaret Court Arena.
  • Together reported at around 6% of group revenue.
  • These are marquee venues — the reputational damage exceeds the revenue.

What they did about it

  • Brad Banducci stepped down at the end of May 2026 after ~14 months.
  • Cameron Hoy took over 1 June and began a restructure.
  • ~44 roles cut in July — 42 in Australia, 2 in the Philippines — about 5.5% of the workforce, to simplify the operating model.
  • A$100M raised from lenders in August to bid for upcoming contracts.
  • Public language: a "refocused growth strategy".

What we do not know

  • Whether service or technology featured in why they lost the tenders.
  • Whether TEG already runs conversational AI, and on what stack.
  • Their actual revenue — public figures conflict wildly.
  • Contact volumes. For a ticketing business these spike enormously around on-sales, which is exactly where human staffing breaks.
  • Whether the ZoomInfo "last touch 6 days ago" signal reflects a real contact by someone at Decagon.
▲ The strategic read

Ticketing is the clearest "spiky volume" use case in DJ's entire territory. Demand is near-zero between on-sales and then vertical for minutes at a time — a shape you cannot staff for and can only automate. TEG has just removed 5.5% of its people while trying to win contracts back from two better-capitalised global competitors. If service capability can be made part of a tender response, that is a revenue argument rather than a cost one, and it is the only account on this list where that framing is the natural one.
